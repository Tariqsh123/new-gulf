    Where Authors in Oman Usually Get Stuck

    The writing is rarely the obstacle. Three things tend to slow people down once the manuscript is done.

    The printing-first trap

    A printed book feels like the finish line, so many authors start by looking for a printer. Then the printer asks for a print-ready file, an ISBN and a cover built to the right spine width, and the real project begins.

    Arabic treated as an add-on

    Arabic titles need right-to-left layouts, suitable typefaces and editors who work in the language natively. When a supplier bolts Arabic on at the end, the problems appear late, after the book has been typeset.

    Rules nobody explains

    Legal deposit, ISBNs and Ministry requirements involve different bodies, and Oman established a National Library in 2026. It is hard to know what applies to your book, or whom to ask.

    A price that moves between calls

    Behind all three sits the question of price. The usual answer is “it depends”, followed by a number that changes with each conversation. We publish ours, itemised.

    Know the Omani Rules Before You Print

    Oman has its own printing and publishing requirements, and 2026 brought a new National Library of Oman. We check what applies to your title, help with the ISBN and deposit paperwork, and flag any Ministry steps before you commit to a print run. Treat this as practical help rather than legal advice. We confirm the current requirements with you first.

    ISBN and the National Library

    A National Library of Oman was established in 2026, and the country has its own ISBN and deposit processes. We guide you through the application and check the number appears correctly on your copyright page and back cover. [Confirm which body issues ISBNs for Oman authors]

    Legal deposit

    Oman has legal deposit rules under its printing and publishing law. We tell you what applies to your book and guide you through the steps.

    Amazon asks authors outside the US to complete a tax interview before paying royalties. We walk you through each question, and you submit it yourself in your own account. For advice on your own tax position, speak to an accountant.
