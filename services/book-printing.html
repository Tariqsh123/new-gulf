    Why Authors Choose a Publisher Over a Print Shop

    A print shop prints the files you hand over. If the spine is the wrong width, the margins are too tight or the cover is in the wrong colour mode, you find out after the run. We work backwards from the printer’s requirements: spine width calculated from page count and paper, bleed and safe margins set, fonts embedded, images at print resolution and the barcode area kept clear.

    The same team that designs your book prepares the files, orders the proof and, if you want, publishes the book through retailers. You deal with one named project manager and approve each stage, so printing becomes a planned step rather than a gamble. Because Arabic and English work are under one roof, bilingual and Arabic titles are handled properly from the first file.

    Print-on-Demand or a Short Run?

    Print-on-demand prints a copy only when someone orders it, so there is no stock to store and no money tied up in a print run. It suits first books, titles sold mainly online and books you want to test. The cost per copy is higher, but you pay only for what sells.

    A short print run produces a batch of copies at once, usually at a lower cost per copy. It suits launches, signings, gifts, schools and organisations that need books in hand. Many authors combine both: a short run for the launch, then print-on-demand afterwards. We recommend a mix based on your quantity and budget. [Confirm minimum quantities for short runs]

    Paperback, Hardcover and Binding Options Explained

    Paperbacks are perfect bound, with a glued spine and a laminated cover. Hardcovers are case bound, cost more and last longer; on Amazon KDP they are a separate edition with their own cover file and are not eligible for expanded distribution. Thin booklets can be saddle stitched, and workbooks can use spiral binding. [Confirm binding options offered]

    Interior paper is usually white for non-fiction and illustrated books and cream for fiction, and colour interiors cost more per page than black and white. Covers can be matte or gloss laminated. [Confirm finishes offered, such as spot UV or foil] Trim size, page count and paper decide the spine width, which is why we fix them before the cover is finished.

    Arabic and Bilingual Book Printing

    Arabic books are reverse bound: the spine sits on the right and the pages open to the left. The cover wrap is mirrored, with the front cover to the left of the spine, and margins, running heads and page numbers mirror too. Amazon KDP prints Arabic as an eBook only, so Arabic paperbacks go through a print route that supports reverse binding, such as IngramSpark or a local printer. [Confirm print route]

    Print also exposes details a screen hides, such as diacritics clipped at the margin or a title that breaks awkwardly across lines. We recommend a printed proof for every Arabic title, reviewed by our native Arabic editors, and Gulf Books Publisher can print bilingual editions too.

    The Printed Proof: Hold the Book Before It Goes Live

    A PDF cannot show how paper feels, how colours land or whether the spine lines up. A printed proof is a physical copy of your book made before the run or the launch. We check colour, spine alignment, margins and gutter, image sharpness and paper, you check the content, and nothing proceeds until you approve it in writing. The full publishing and launch package includes a printed proof, and a proof can be added to any project.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the difference between print-on-demand and a print run?

    Print-on-demand prints single copies as they are ordered, with no stock. A print run produces a batch at once, usually at a lower cost per copy. Gulf Books Publisher can recommend the right mix for your book.

    Can you print hardcover as well as paperback?

    Yes. Hardcover is a separate edition with its own cover file and costs more per copy. We prepare the files and a proof for either format. [Confirm hardcover options]

    Can I print an Arabic book through Amazon KDP?

    Not as a paperback. KDP prints Arabic as an eBook only, so we prepare Arabic paperbacks for a reverse-binding print route such as IngramSpark or a local printer. [Confirm]

    Do I need an ISBN to print my book?

    Not for private copies. You need an ISBN to sell through bookstores, libraries and most online retailers. We register your ISBN and explain the steps for your country.

    Can I order a printed proof before the full run?

    Yes, and we recommend it. A printed proof lets you check colour, spine and margins before the run. It is included in the full publishing and launch package and can be added to any project.

    How much does book printing cost in the UAE and the Gulf?

    Cost depends on trim size, page count, paper, colour, binding and quantity. Your itemised quote shows it before work starts. [Add per-copy rates or ranges]
