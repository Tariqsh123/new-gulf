Why Authors Hire a Ghostwriter

A ghostwriter writes the book, an editor improves a book you have already written, and a co-author shares the credit. With ghostwriting, your name goes on the cover and the words carry your voice. Authors usually come to us for one of three reasons: they know the subject but not how to structure a book, they have the time to talk but not to write, or they started a draft and could not finish it.

Template tools and quick marketplace gigs produce generic text, because nobody has listened to you first. We start with conversation. Your writer asks questions, records your answers, studies how you speak and write, and drafts chapters you recognise as yours. Because writing, editing, cover design, formatting and publishing sit under one roof, the manuscript moves forward without a hand-off to a second supplier. You have a named project manager, and you approve every stage.

Ghostwriter, Editor or Co-Author: Which Do You Need?

If you have no draft, you need a ghostwriter. If you have a complete manuscript that needs polishing, you need an editor, and our editing service covers that at several levels. If you want a named partner who shares the byline and the royalties, you are looking for a co-author, which is a different agreement. Many authors start with a ghostwriter for the first draft and move to editing for the final pass. Tell us where you are, and we will recommend the simplest route that gets the book finished.

What a Ghostwriter Actually Does

Ghostwriting is more than typing up your notes. It begins with discovery interviews, held by video, phone or WhatsApp, where your writer draws out stories, arguments and details you may not have thought to include. From those, they build a chapter plan, research any facts that need checking and match your voice: vocabulary, rhythm, humour and level of formality.

Drafting happens in stages, so you read early chapters, give feedback and steer the book before it grows. You do not have to write a word, though you can share notes, voice memos, speeches, slides or rough chapters, and your writer will use them. The result is a complete manuscript that sounds like you on your best day.

Arabic and English Ghostwriting

Arabic ghostwriting is not English ghostwriting translated. Our native Arabic writers work in Arabic from the first interview, so the voice is natural rather than converted. They judge when a passage calls for Modern Standard Arabic and when Gulf dialect belongs in dialogue, handle names, quotations and honorifics with care, and keep the tone right for your readers, whether the book is a memoir, a business title or a children’s story.

English manuscripts are written to the spelling convention you prefer, British or American, with attention to voice rather than a stock style. Gulf Books Publisher keeps Arabic and English writing, editing and formatting under one roof, so an author who wants both languages works with one team.
