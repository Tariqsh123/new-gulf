    Amazon KDP Explained: Paperback, Hardcover and eBook

    Amazon KDP (Kindle Direct Publishing) lets you sell your book worldwide without printing stock in advance. Paperbacks and hardcovers are printed on demand, which means a copy is made only when a reader orders it, so you carry no inventory and no upfront print run. eBooks are read on Kindle devices and apps.

    Each format has its own rules. Print books need exact trim sizes, margins, a bleed area where images reach the page edge, and a cover template that matches your page count. eBooks need clean, reflowable files with a working table of contents. Royalties differ by format and price, and Amazon sets conditions for each, so we explain the current options before you choose a price. You can also decide whether to enrol your eBook in KDP Select, which offers promotion tools in exchange for a period of Amazon exclusivity. We set out the trade-offs so the choice is yours.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the difference between book publishing and Amazon KDP?

    KDP is Amazon’s platform for selling your book. Book publishing is the work around it: formatting, cover design, ISBN and metadata. Gulf Books Publisher handles both, so your book is ready for KDP and set up correctly.

    Do I keep the rights to my book?

    Yes. We recommend publishing through your own KDP account, so your rights, royalties and reviews stay with you. We prepare and upload the files, and you approve everything before your book goes live.

    Can you publish Arabic books on Amazon KDP?

    Yes, subject to KDP’s current language and format support, which we check for your title first. Native Arabic editors and designers handle right-to-left layout, punctuation and fonts, and we can also prepare bilingual editions.

    Do I need my own ISBN?

    Not always. A free KDP ISBN works only on KDP, while your own ISBN is portable and lets you name your own imprint. We explain both options and set up whichever suits your plans.
