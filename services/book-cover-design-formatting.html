What a Book Cover Includes, Explained

A printed cover is one wraparound image with three working parts. The front cover carries the title, subtitle and author name, and has to stay readable as a small thumbnail on a phone. The spine carries the title, author and publisher mark; Amazon KDP requires at least 79 pages before spine text is allowed. The back cover holds the blurb, an author note, review quotes and a clear area for the ISBN barcode.

Behind the artwork sits the technical build: bleed beyond the trim edge, safe margins for text, a spine width worked out from page count and paper type, print-resolution images and the colour mode your printer asks for. KDP expects the front, spine and back as a single PDF. An eBook cover is simpler, one front image sized for retailer listings, but it still has to stay sharp on small screens.

Book Formatting Explained: Interior Layout, Typesetting and eBook Files

Formatting turns a manuscript into a book. For print, we set the trim size, margins and gutter, choose typefaces and line spacing, design chapter openings, add running heads and page numbers, place the front and back matter, and control awkward line and page breaks so pages read cleanly. Images, tables and footnotes are positioned and checked.

For eBooks, we build an EPUB file. Novels and non-fiction normally use a reflowable layout that adapts to the reader’s screen, while picture books and heavily illustrated titles use a fixed layout that keeps every page exactly as designed. Each file is tested in a previewer before submission, so the book you approve is the book readers receive.

Arabic and Bilingual Cover Design and Formatting

Arabic books are bound on the right and open to the left, so the cover and interior must be built in reverse. On a paperback cover file, the front cover sits to the left of the spine and the back cover to the right, and margins, running heads and page numbers mirror accordingly. On the page, we use readable Naskh-style typefaces for body text, reserve calligraphic styles such as Thuluth, Diwani or Kufic for titles, set diacritics only where the text needs them, and keep English names, numerals and web addresses from breaking the right-to-left flow. Arabic eBooks also need the correct right-to-left page-turn setting inside the EPUB.

Our native Arabic editors check titles, blurbs and diacritics before files are released, a step Gulf Books Publisher builds into every Arabic project. One practical point: Amazon KDP publishes Arabic as an eBook only, so Arabic paperbacks go through a print route that supports reverse binding, such as IngramSpark or a local printer. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between cover design and book formatting?

Cover design creates the front, spine and back artwork. Formatting lays out the interior pages and eBook files. Gulf Books Publisher handles both, so the cover and the pages match in style, size and print specification.

Do you design covers and format interiors for Arabic books?

Yes. We build right-to-left interiors and reverse-bound covers, and our native Arabic editors check titles and blurbs. Arabic and English can also be combined in one bilingual edition.

Can I print my Arabic paperback through Amazon KDP?

Not currently. KDP publishes Arabic as an eBook only, so we prepare Arabic paperbacks for a print route that supports reverse binding, such as IngramSpark or a local printer. [Confirm]

Will my cover work for both print and eBook?
